How to Market a Solana Token Without Hype or Shortcuts

A practical launch-marketing plan for Solana tokens — trust signals first, one official address, listings in the right order, honest promotion and steady updates.

DegenKitOctober 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Creating a Solana token takes minutes. Getting people to notice it, and to trust what they see, takes much longer. Most marketing advice for new tokens focuses on attention: more posts, more influencers, more noise. Attention without trust usually fades after the first chart candle.

This guide puts the work in a more durable order: make the token easy to verify, publish one source of truth, get listed where people look, promote honestly, and keep showing up.

Start with trust signals

Before you ask anyone to look at your token, make sure it passes the checks experienced buyers run:

  • Mint authority revoked, so supply cannot grow.
  • Freeze authority revoked, so holders cannot be frozen.
  • Metadata locked, so the name and logo cannot change.
  • A sensible holder distribution, explained if a large wallet is a pool or treasury.
  • Liquidity evidence for any lock or burn you plan to claim.

People will paste your mint address into scanners. DegenKit's Scanner shows these signals for any Solana mint. Run it on your own token first and fix what you can before launch.

Publish one source of truth

Copycat tokens with the same name and logo appear quickly. The only reliable identifier is the mint address. Publish one official launch post that includes:

  1. The full mint address, not a shortened version.
  2. The network: Solana.
  3. Links to your website and every official social account.
  4. Supply, decimals and the state of mint authority, freeze authority and metadata.
  5. Where trading happens and where liquidity evidence can be checked.

Pin that post, link it from your website, and repeat the same address everywhere.

Get listed in the right order

Listings make a token easier to find, but they come after trading starts, not before.

  • DEX Screener shows a pair once it trades. According to its help center, token descriptions and social links come from external token lists such as CoinGecko's, or from its Enhanced Token Info product.
  • CoinGecko requires a token to be actively tradable on an exchange it tracks before you apply. It also warns that "When list?" spam can disqualify a project.

Fill in every listing form with the same name, symbol, address and links as your launch post. Mismatches slow reviews and confuse buyers.

Skip the shortcuts

Fake volume, bought followers and wash trading may look like traction for a day. They are easy to detect and can permanently damage a project's reputation. In many places they can also break the law.

Promote honestly

Paid promotion is common in crypto. Hidden paid promotion is the problem. The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's guidance for influencers says an endorsement should make it obvious when there is a material connection, such as payment or free products. Rules differ by country, but disclosure is a safe default everywhere.

When you work with creators or communities:

  • Ask them to disclose that they were paid or received tokens.
  • Give them the official address and launch post, not just a ticker.
  • Never let anyone promise returns, price targets or "guaranteed" gains.

If you run an airdrop, tell recipients what they are receiving and why. Unexplained tokens look like spam, and many wallets hide them.

Keep showing up

The first week gets the most attention. The following months decide whether anyone stays. A simple routine helps:

  • Post updates on a predictable schedule, even when the news is small.
  • Share on-chain evidence for every claim: burns, liquidity changes, wallet moves.
  • Answer the same questions in a pinned FAQ instead of in scattered replies.
  • Correct mistakes publicly and quickly.

Consistency is less exciting than a viral post, but it is what makes a project look maintained.

Questions people ask

Should we pay for a listing upgrade on day one?

Only if the basics are finished. A polished listing page cannot fix revoked-authority questions or missing liquidity evidence.

Do we need a website?

A simple one-page site with the official address and links goes a long way. It gives listing reviewers and new holders one place to check.

Wrap up

Good token marketing starts before the first post: make the token verifiable, publish one official address, get listed after trading begins, disclose paid promotion and keep showing up.
